Week 1 – 5S Activity and Portfolio Development

The first week of the internship began with a hands-on introduction to workplace discipline through a 5S workplace organization activity, which ran across the first three days. This exercise was designed to help us understand how a clean, systematic, and well-labeled workspace directly contributes to efficiency and safety in a real industrial or lab environment — a principle that is widely used across manufacturing and engineering setups.

I was assigned to the Mechanical Team, and our specific responsibility was to sort and organize the wide variety of cables scattered around the workspace into clearly labeled boxes based on their type, size, and function. On the very first day, we made significant progress by carefully identifying each cable type, grouping similar ones together, and arranging them in a logical, easy-to-locate manner. This required a good deal of attention to detail, since mislabeling or disorganizing the cables at this stage would have created confusion later when someone needed to quickly find a specific type.

On the second day, having already completed the bulk of the cable-sorting work, I continued supporting my own team by helping finish the remaining organizational tasks. This included double-checking labels, refining the arrangement, and making sure everything was consistent across the boxes. Working through this process as a team gave me a much clearer appreciation for how collaboration and communication play a critical role in keeping a shared workspace functional — even a task as seemingly simple as sorting cables becomes far more effective when everyone is aligned and pulling in the same direction.

By the third day, our team moved on to the final stage of the activity: neatly arranging all the organized cable boxes inside the storage cabinet. We made sure everything was placed in a way that was both visually tidy and practically accessible, so that anyone needing a particular cable in the future could locate it quickly without having to search through unlabeled clutter. This final step really brought the 5S principles full circle — Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize, and Sustain — and demonstrated in a very tangible way how much of a difference proper organization makes to the overall efficiency of a workspace.

With the organizational work wrapped up, the remaining two days of the week shifted focus toward a completely different but equally important task: building our personal portfolios. This involved thinking carefully about how to present our educational background, technical skills, completed and ongoing projects, key achievements, and career objectives in a clear, professional, and visually organized format. Beyond just compiling information, this exercise pushed us to reflect on how we wanted to represent ourselves professionally — what strengths to highlight, what projects best showcased our abilities, and how to communicate our goals in a way that would resonate with future recruiters or collaborators. It was a valuable exercise in self-presentation, something that often gets overlooked in technical training but is equally essential for career growth.

Looking back, Week 1 turned out to be a well-rounded introduction to both the practical and professional sides of the internship. On one hand, the 5S activity reinforced the discipline, teamwork, and attention to detail required to maintain an efficient physical workspace. On the other, the portfolio-building exercise helped sharpen how I think about and present my own skills and experiences. Together, these two halves of the week gave me a strong foundation — both in terms of workplace habits and personal branding — to carry forward into the rest of the seminar.
