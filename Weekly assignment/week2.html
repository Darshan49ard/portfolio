Week 2

From Ideas to Applications: Coding, Design, and Building

This week marked a shift from structure and workplace discipline to creativity, design thinking, and practical problem-solving. Through lectures, interactive activities, and small hands-on tasks, I began to connect ideas with real-world application and saw how innovation can emerge from constraints, teamwork, and curiosity.

  • Learned about frugal innovation and how limited resources can still create meaningful solutions.
  • Participated in the GIE Rubrics Evaluation, which strengthened observation, critical thinking, and teamwork.
  • Explored design thinking and coding basics to improve problem-solving and creative confidence.
  • Started turning simple ideas into structured and practical applications.

Big takeaway

Real innovation is not about having more tools or resources — it is about using ideas, discipline, and creativity effectively to solve the right problem.

Day 1: Frugal Innovation and Team Building

The week began with an auditorium session led by Prof. Mukesh Sud, who spoke about his book, Lean Spark: Frugal by Design, Global in Impact. His talk highlighted how innovation often arises not from abundance, but from constraints, creativity, and disciplined thinking. The session gave me a fresh perspective on how resourcefulness and design can lead to meaningful solutions in real life.

In the afternoon, we took part in the GIE Rubrics Evaluation. This activity pushed us to observe patterns, think critically, and work together as a team while evaluating quality, structure, and performance criteria. It was a practical reminder that problem-solving is not only about individual intelligence, but also about collaboration, communication, and staying calm under pressure.

The combination of the lecture and the activity made the day very valuable. It helped me understand that innovation is rooted not only in technical expertise but also in mindset, creativity, and teamwork. By the end of the day, I had a clearer idea of how ideas can become structured solutions when supported by good thinking and strong cooperation.

Day 2–5: Design Thinking and Creative Execution

As the week progressed, the focus moved toward design thinking and project-based learning. We worked on tasks that required us to organize our ideas, approach problems in a systematic way, and think about how concepts can be shaped into useful applications. This helped me move beyond passive learning and begin understanding the process of turning an idea into a practical outcome.

The sessions reinforced the importance of structured thinking. Instead of jumping directly to a solution, we learned to observe, analyze, test, and refine our approach. This mindset is especially useful in engineering and innovation, where clear reasoning and iterative improvement are essential for success.

By the end of the week, I felt more confident in my ability to think creatively while still staying disciplined and structured. It was a week of learning that balanced concept-building with practical execution, and it gave me a strong foundation for the weeks ahead.
