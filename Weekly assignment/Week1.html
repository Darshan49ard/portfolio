Week 1

5S Activity and Portfolio Development

The first week focused on hands-on discipline, organization, and self-presentation. As part of the Defab team, I worked on sorting mixed fasteners, learning the basics of laser cutting, and organizing workshop tools and mechanical instruments while also building my portfolio to present my skills and goals.

  • Worked in the Defab team to sort and arrange mixed nuts, bolts, and fasteners by type and size.
  • Learned the basics of laser cutting machines and how precision work is handled in a workshop.
  • Helped organize and separate power tools and mechanical instruments to improve accessibility and safety.
  • Built a professional portfolio to present my skills, project interests, and career goals.

Key learning

A clean and well-organized workspace improves productivity, safety, and teamwork. The same principle applies not only to workshop organization but also to personal branding and career growth.

The first week of the internship began with a hands-on work experience in the Defab team, where we focused on organizing and improving the workshop environment. Our main responsibility was to sort and arrange the mixed nuts, bolts, and other small mechanical fasteners that had been scattered around the workspace. This required careful observation, grouping by type and size, and making sure each item was placed in an orderly and easy-to-access location. It was a practical introduction to the importance of organization, proper labeling, and maintaining a safe and efficient workspace.

As part of the same activity, we also worked on sorting all the power tools and mechanical instruments used in the lab. This included identifying which tools belonged together, separating items that were mixed, and arranging them in a systematic layout so that they could be easily found and used later. This taught us how much efficiency and safety improve when tools are organized, labeled, and kept in a consistent place. It also showed how teamwork matters in a shared workshop environment, because every member had to pay attention to detail and maintain the same standard of order.

In addition to the sorting work, we were introduced to the basics of the laser cutting machine. We learned how it is used in fabrication and manufacturing, how precision is important in cutting work, and how the machine must be handled carefully to achieve accurate results. Even though it was only a basic introduction, it gave us a clearer understanding of how modern fabrication processes work and why accuracy, preparation, and maintenance are essential in a mechanical workshop.

The final part of the week shifted toward portfolio development, where we worked on presenting our personal background, skills, interests, and career goals in a professional format. This helped us reflect on what we had learned so far and how we wanted to present ourselves in future academic and professional settings. By combining hands-on workshop learning with self-presentation practice, the week gave us both technical and personal growth.

Looking back, Week 1 turned out to be a well-rounded introduction to both the practical and professional sides of the internship. On one hand, the 5S activity reinforced the discipline, teamwork, and attention to detail required to maintain an efficient physical workspace. On the other, the portfolio-building exercise helped sharpen how I think about and present my own skills and experiences. Together, these two halves of the week gave me a strong foundation — both in terms of workplace habits and personal branding — to carry forward into the rest of the seminar.
